# v0.18 Briefing: Design

**Status:** written 2026-11-03 from the owner's choices in that day's planning session. The owner approved the manual's mock website and Figma frames G1, E1, E2, R1, O1 and L1 on 2026-11-03 and started development; D15–D18 are shown in what they approved, and the rest of §13 is built with its defaults. Numbers are starting values to tune in play. Nothing is built yet; the step-by-step implementation plan goes in `superpowers/plans/` when the update starts (after v0.16 and v0.17 have had their Linux matches).
**Parent:** [roadmap](../../roadmap.md) §2 v0.18. **Figma brief:** [ux/v0.18-figma.md](../../ux/v0.18-figma.md).

## 1. Goal

Show players what they need to know, and take the friction out of everyday actions:

1. **Knowing the game:** a **Field manual** that explains every unit, building and rule, generated from `GameConfig.xml` (§2).
2. **Reading the battle:** **range and sight rings** while a key is held, and the reach of a Gun turret, Radar or Jammer while placing it (§3).
3. **Looking back:** **end-screen graphs** and a **match timeline** (§4), and a **replay viewer** with the same graphs, markers on its seek bar and a **per-player view** through that player's fog (§5).
4. **Ease of use:** an **Attack order** (right-click or A-click on an enemy), a **drag box that prefers the army** over Builders (§6), and **Rematch** plus **remembered lobby settings** (§7).
5. **Suicide bombers** (owner addition, 2026-11-03): a bomber brought down to 10 % health explodes, and bombers aim for the middle of an army or a building instead of its edge (§6.3). This is the update's one balance change.

**Out of scope** (owner, 2026-11-03): status markers over units and buildings, richer hover cards (the manual carries the detail; the cards stay as they are), an economy breakdown, a production overview, a live stats screen, an always-on waypoint view, Patrol and repeat production, a hotkey overlay, a tutorial, panel-click selection tricks, squad add/steal, select damaged/idle, mixed production queues, camera bookmarks, counters on hover cards. Nothing else from the owner's 2026-10-30 "Not pursued" list ([polish.md](../../polish.md)): only **end-screen graphs** come back from it.

**The leak rule:** §2, §3 and §6.2 are drawn only from what the client already has (its config, its own selection, replicated units and buildings). The Attack order (§6.1) and a bomber's aim (§6.3) only ever act on what the owner's side sees now. The graphs and timeline (§4) follow the match statistics' rule: sent to everyone only at _GameOver_ (`StatsRules.MaySend`). Replays stay on the host (§5). `LeakTests` gain the tests named below and the rest pass unchanged.

## 2. The Field manual

### 2.1 What it is

A reference screen with five sections:

| Section | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Units** | A card per `UnitType` (its atlas sprite in the player's colour, name, one-line role from `HoverText.UnitRole`), opening a page: what it is for (two or three sentences), every stat (cost, build time, upkeep, health, armour, damage and interval, damage per second, range and minimum range, sight, speed, size class, splash or blast), which building makes it, its **damage-table row** (vs light units, heavy units, buildings, walls) and **Strong against / Weak against**, worked out from the damage table (§2.3). |
| **Buildings** | A card per `BuildingType`, opening a page: what it does, size, cost, health, upkeep, sight, what it makes (from `SpawnerRules.Produces`) and the special numbers (a Miner's yield on gems and rich gems, a Radar's range, a Jammer's radius, a Gun turret's weapon), and that it starts as a blueprint Builders build. Walls and Bombs have pages too. |
| **Damage table** | The whole `<DamageTable>` as a grid, units and the Gun turret down the side, target classes across, multipliers coloured from weak to strong; hovering a cell gives the effective damage per shot and per second. |
| **Rules** | Short illustrated pages: the economy (passive income, Miners, upkeep and decay), fog of war, teams and diplomacy, siege (walls, digging, bombs), winning and losing. Each number is the config's. |
| **Controls** | Every binding as it is set now (read from the player's settings), grouped like Settings → Controls, with a button to Settings → Controls. Read-only. |

A search box filters cards and pages by name. The manual remembers the last page opened (per machine, as a setting-like convenience).

### 2.2 Where it opens

- **Main menu:** a **Manual** button beside Replays.
- **Lobby:** a **Manual** button (for anyone, host or not), opening over the lobby; the lobby carries on behind it (a ready change or a kick still shows).
- **In-match menu (Esc):** a **Manual** entry between Settings and Surrender.
- **In a match:** **F1** (rebindable: "Field manual") opens it straight to the page of the **selected** unit or building type (the first type in the selection panel's order; no selection opens the last page). F1 or Esc closes it.
- **Pause:** opening it in a match counts as the in-match menu for the pause rule (v0.14): when you are the only human it pauses the match, otherwise the match runs on behind it.

### 2.3 Generated, never stale

- Every number comes from `ConfigLoader.LoadConfig()` at the moment the manual opens (no literals in the manual's text). The words live in one pure class, `ManualText` (like `HoverText` and `LobbySettingHelp`), with placeholders the numbers fill.
- **Strong / Weak against** (pure, `ManualRules.Counters`; changed 2026-11-03, D15): one signed score per pair, from an equal-cost fight on open ground (the model in `tools/balance-sheet.js`: range, minimum range, splash and numbers; 3,000 resources a side), run both ways round and averaged, so `score(a, b) = -score(b, a)` and a matchup can't read as both strong and weak. A page lists up to three wins and three losses with a margin of at least 20; Builders and Diggers aren't named under "Strong against". The Gun turret takes part like a unit. A Suicide bomber has no gun for that fight, so it is ranked by the worth one blast takes from a packed group of the target, after what the target shoots down on the way in; its top three targets and bottom three count as losses and wins for them. A Builder has no lists. The first rule (damage per second over health per cost) gave nearly every unit the same lists and put the Sniper on both of the Tank's.
- **Tests** (EditMode): every `UnitType` and `BuildingType` has a page and a card (a new type without text fails the build, as `ArtManifest` does for art); every number on every page matches the config (pages are rendered with a test config whose numbers are all distinct primes, and each is found in the text); the counters read one way everywhere (no pair strong both ways, none both strong and weak) and match `tools/manual-mock.js` for the shipped config; the damage grid has every cell.

### 2.4 Approval first: a mock website (owner, 2026-11-03)

Nothing of the manual is built into the game until the owner has **approved a mock website of it**. The mock is the manual's content and structure in full, so it can be read and judged page by page before any UI Toolkit work starts:

- **What:** a static web page (one HTML file, published as a private artifact link for the owner) with all five sections, every unit and building page, the damage grid, the rules pages and a Controls page with the default bindings. Search, the section switcher and page links work, and it uses the game's look (Jersey 10, the `Theme` colours, square corners) so the layout reads like the real thing.
- **Generated, not hand-typed:** a script (`tools/manual-mock.js`, Node, like `balance-sheet.js`) reads `Assets/Resources/GameConfig.xml` and writes the page, so every number and the Strong / Weak against lists are the ones the game would show, worked out with the same rule as `ManualRules.Counters` (§2.3). The script refuses to write the page if the manual contradicts itself: a matchup reading both ways, words that disagree with the fights (each page's `claims`), a role line that differs from `HoverText.UnitRole`, a number typed into the words, or a type without text. Unit and building sprites come from the built atlases where available, else a placeholder box with the name.
- **The words** of every page are drafted in the mock (one source file the script reads, `tools/manual-text.json`), reviewed by the owner there, and then carried into `ManualText` as approved. A test compares `ManualText` with the approved file so the two can't drift while building.
- **Review loop:** the owner comments on the mock (the artifact's comments, or in conversation); it is regenerated and republished until approved. The approval and its date are recorded in this spec and in the [Figma brief](../../ux/v0.18-figma.md).
- **Then** the in-game look is drawn in Figma (F1–F3) from the approved mock, and only after that is approved is the manual built.

### 2.5 Look

UI Toolkit (`Manual.uxml`, `ManualController`, `ManualModel`), the same look as the v0.16 menus (Jersey 10, square corners, `Theme` and `Scale`), Reduce motion respected. Figma frames F1–F3.

## 3. Range and sight rings

- **Hold to show:** while **Alt** is held (rebindable: "Show ranges"), every selected own unit and Gun turret shows its **attack range** (a thin solid ring), its **minimum range** if it has one (Artillery: a dashed inner ring) and its **sight** (a faint dotted ring); a selected Radar or Jammer shows its radar range or jam radius. The unit or building **under the pointer** (own, allied, or an enemy the client sees, ghosts included) shows the same rings, in its owner's colour. A unit type's ranges are public knowledge (they are in everyone's config), so this sends nothing new.
- **Cap:** at most `RangeRings/Max` (64) rings sets, those nearest the view's centre first. Units of one type standing close share one ring when their circles would overlap by more than 80 % (`RangeRings/MergeOverlap`), so a blob of 200 Tanks draws a handful of rings, not 200.
- **While placing** a Gun turret, Radar or Jammer, its reach is always drawn round the placement preview (no key needed): the turret's range, the Radar's range, the Jammer's radius. The rest of the placement preview is unchanged.
- **Drawn** with the existing ring overlay (the Jammer ring and rally flag code in `Client/Overlays/`), crisp at every zoom (1 px or whole art pixels), under the HUD. No setting: the key is the setting.
- **Tests:** ring choice (which units, the cap, merging), the placement reach per building type, and a hovered enemy's rings drawn from its type alone.

## 4. End-screen graphs and timeline

### 4.1 Data (server)

`GameCore.Stats` (`MatchStats`) gains a time series and a timeline, filled during _Playing_ only and on the match clock (`GameCore.MatchClock`, so a pause adds nothing):

- **Every `Summary/SampleSeconds` (10 s)**, at the boundary that already counts peak army, per player: **army value** (the summed `Cost` of their living units), **income** in that interval (passive income plus Miner payouts, not gifts; shown per minute), and the cumulative **units killed** and **units lost** (from the existing counters).
- **At most `Summary/MaxPoints` (360) samples per player** (an hour at 10 s). When full, neighbouring pairs merge (averages for income, the later value for the rest) and the interval doubles, so a long match keeps its whole shape.
- **Timeline events:**
  - **Eliminations:** time, player, and how (HQ destroyed, surrendered, left, connection timed out), and who destroyed the HQ when the last hit is known (`Unit.LastHitBy`).
  - **Big fights:** unit deaths (`SimContext.UnitKilled`, with the dead unit's tile) are binned into `Summary/FightCellTiles` (32) cells. A fight **opens** when the cost of units killed in one cell (with its 8 neighbours) within `Summary/FightWindowSeconds` (20) reaches `Summary/FightMinWorth` (600), and **closes** after a window with no deaths there. It records start and end time, the centre (cost-weighted mean of the deaths), and per player the units and worth lost and killed in it. At most `Summary/MaxFights` (30) per match, keeping the largest by total worth lost.
- **Sending:** with the existing statistics, in `RpcMatchStats`, only at _GameOver_ (`StatsRules.MaySend`). With 8 players × 360 samples × 4 values plus 30 fights it is under 60 KB; if it ever passes Mirror's message limit it goes in chunks (a test sends the largest case).

### 4.2 The end screen

The end screen (`EndScreenController`) gets three tabs: **Summary** (today's table, unchanged), **Graphs** and **Timeline**.

- **Graphs:** one line chart at a time, picked by buttons: Army value, Income (per minute), Units killed, Units lost. One line per player in their colour (the colour palette setting applies), the local player's line thicker, a legend that toggles players, a hover read-out with each player's value at that time. Eliminations are marked on the time axis.
- **Timeline:** the eliminations and big fights in time order ("12:40 · Big fight near the middle: you lost 1,240, IronFox lost 2,010"), each with the players involved. A fight's place is described by its position on the map (centre, north-west, near <player>'s base when within 24 tiles of an HQ that player had) rather than by coordinates.
- Drawn with UI Toolkit (`Painter2D` for the lines), no new package. Figma frames E1–E2.

### 4.3 Tests

Sampling and merging (`SummaryTests`: a 90-minute match keeps 360 points and its peaks), income per interval with a pause in it, the fight detector (one fight across two cells, two fights in one cell separated by a quiet window, a skirmish under the threshold, the cap keeps the largest), elimination causes, the message size at its maximum, and that nothing of it leaves the server before _GameOver_ (`LeakTests.SummaryIsSentOnlyAtGameOver`).

## 5. Replay viewer

### 5.1 Recording (format 3)

`ReplayFile.Format` becomes **3**; format 2 files are refused as today ("replay format 2 is not supported"), and a protocol bump refuses them anyway. Two new channels:

- **Summary:** the §4 graphs and timeline, written once at _GameOver_ (before the recorder's tail), so the viewer has them from the start of playback (it reads the summary record first; the file is read once to the end when it opens, which the seek bar already needs for its length).
- **Team fog:** for each team (more exactly each distinct vision set: a player plus everyone sharing vision with them, as `SimVisionSystem` computes), the changed fog cells (seen now / explored), every `Replays/FogSeconds` (1 s), run-length encoded. The grids are the server's own (radar, jammers, shared vision and diplomacy changes included), so the per-player view is exact, not recomputed. Encoded on the recorder's `DeferredLane` like its unit records, so the boundary stays inside the perf budget.
- **Size:** fog deltas on a 1024 map with 8 players are measured in the perf run; `Replays/MaxMegabytes` still caps a file, and a recording that would pass it drops its fog channel first (the replay still plays, with the per-player view unavailable and saying why).

### 5.2 The viewer

- **Graphs:** a Graphs button opens the §4.2 chart over the viewer, with a vertical line at the current playback time.
- **Seek-bar markers:** each elimination (a skull) and big fight (crossed swords, longer for a longer fight) sits on the seek bar at its time; hovering says what it was, clicking seeks to `Replays/MarkerLeadSeconds` (5) before it and centres the camera on it.
- **Per-player view:** a **View** selector: Everything (today's view, the default) or any player. Choosing a player draws that player's team's fog from the recorded grids, hides other teams' units outside the cells that team sees now, shows other teams' buildings as that team last saw them (the viewer keeps the ghost memory as it plays forward; seeking back replays from the start, as it already does, so the memory stays exact), and never shows another team's bombs. The player panel highlights the chosen player and their resources.
- Figma frame R1.

### 5.3 Tests

The fog channel round-trips (a recorded team grid equals the server's at the same tick), the per-player view's visibility of units, ghosts and bombs against the recorded grid (`ReplayViewTests`), the oversize fallback, markers' seek times, and format 2 refused.

## 6. Orders, selection and Suicide bombers

### 6.1 The Attack order

- **What:** **right-click on an enemy** unit or building with units selected, or an armed **Attack-move clicked on an enemy**, orders a targeted attack: the units go after that target, fire at it whenever it is in their range, and ignore other enemies while they do (like a Move). When the target dies the order ends and they stand where they are (stance Idle: they fight what comes into range). **Shift** queues it like any order (it takes one of the `Orders/MaxQueued` waypoints). Builders and Diggers in the selection keep their v0.7 right-click (`ClickOrders`); everything else that can attack takes the Attack. Units that can't damage the target (none today) move instead.
- **"Enemy"** means an owner on the sender's attack mask (`SimData.AttackMask`), never just a different owner. A right-click on an ally's or your own unit is a Move, as today. An enemy wall keeps its v0.7 behaviour (attack-move onto it).
- **Wire:** `OrderKind.Attack = 8`; `CmdOrderChunk` and `CmdOrderSquad` gain an `int targetId` (0 for every other order). The server checks it through `CommandGate` and `CommandValidator` as it does the rest: the id names a live unit or a building, its owner is on the sender's attack mask, and it is **visible to the sender's side now** (a unit on a fog cell the team sees, a building the team sees, not a ghost). If any check fails the order becomes an **Attack-move to the clicked tile**, exactly what the client could have sent itself, so a refusal tells it nothing.
- **The fog rule (leak):** the attackers follow the target only while their side sees it. When it leaves sight, the order's goal stays at the **last tile their side saw it on**; they walk there, and if it isn't seen on arrival the order ends. The server never routes a unit using a hidden target's position: units' routes reach their owner's client, so that would draw the hidden target's path. `LeakTests.AnAttackOrderNeverFollowsATargetIntoTheFog` and `...NeverNamesATargetTheSideCannotSee`.
- **Sim:** a new per-unit array `OrderTarget` (the target's id, 0 for none) in `SimData`, written only at the boundary. All units ordered onto one target share one order slot (`OrderBook`) whose goal **follows the target**: at the boundary, when the visible target has moved more than `AttackOrder/RepathTiles` (2) from the slot's goal and the slot hasn't moved for `AttackOrder/RepathSeconds` (0.5), the goal moves to it (one flow field per target, not per unit). The combat stage's target search prefers the order's target when it is in range and in line of sight, and picks nothing else while the order holds; a unit in range of its target stops and fires rather than walking on to the goal. Attack orders never form up (formations are for Move and Attack-move).
- **Client:** the cursor over an enemy with attackers selected is the attack cursor (the existing `CursorAttack`, with a dark outline if D16 is accepted; Figma O1); a selected unit on an Attack order draws a **solid** target line to its target (§2.1 of v0.16 kept the dashed one for attack-move goals) from `Selection`'s memory of the orders it sent, until the target is gone or the order replaced. The Attack-move hover card gains a line: "Click an enemy to attack it".
- **AI:** unchanged in v0.18 (its focus fire stays an attack-move, off at every difficulty); see D9.
- **Tests:** `SimAttackOrderTests` (chases and kills a moving target; ignores a nearer enemy; stands when the target dies; the shared slot repaths and only after the threshold; walks to the last seen tile and ends when the target slips into fog; a building target; a queued Attack after a Move; an ally or invisible target becomes an attack-move), the command's validation (bad ids, wrong owner, ghosts), the two `LeakTests`, and `ClickOrders` for the right-click split.

### 6.2 The drag box prefers the army

- A drag box (plain or Shift+drag) that catches **Builders and any other own unit** selects only the others; a box that catches **only Builders** selects them as today. Diggers and Suicide bombers count as army (see D5). Buildings are untouched (a box that catches units already ignores buildings).
- No override key (owner, 2026-11-03): `.` selects idle Builders, Ctrl + `.` every Builder, double-click a Builder all Builders on screen, and a box with only Builders in it still works.
- Client only (`Selection`), pure rule `SelectionRules.PreferArmy` with tests; Shift+drag applies it to what the box adds, not to what was already selected.

### 6.3 Suicide bombers (owner, 2026-11-03)

Today a bomber runs at the **nearest** enemy unit or building and explodes within 0.8 tiles of its **edge** (`SimCombatSystem.BomberJob.InContact`); one shot down first never explodes ([gameplay.md](../../gameplay.md) "Suicide bomber"). Both change.

**Last gasp: a bomber at 10 % health explodes.**

- A bomber whose health falls to `Bomber/LastGaspHealth` (0.1) of its maximum or below explodes where it stands, with its full blast (`BlastRadius` 2, `BlastDamage` 150, friends included, as now). This includes a hit that **kills** it (see D12): a bomber has 30 health, so the threshold is 3, and every attacker's single hit on a light unit except a Digger's (2) is 4 or more. A rule for survivors only would almost never fire; in practice this rule means **a bomber that is shot down explodes**. Decay (unpaid upkeep, 1.5 a second) also sets it off once it reaches 3.
- **Where:** a `LastGaspJob` after `ApplyDamageJob` in the combat stage finds bombers that crossed the threshold this tick (above it at the start of the tick, at or below it now) and detonates them through the same `Detonate` code, then applies their hits. A bomber caught in that blast and pushed under the threshold explodes in the next pass, up to `Bomber/ChainPasses` (4) passes in one tick; anything left over goes off on the next tick if it is still alive. Credit for kills goes to the bomber's owner, as for any blast.
- **What it changes:** shooting a bomber down is still the counter, but where it dies matters: Snipers (range 16), Artillery (14) and turrets (7) kill it well clear of what they protect, while a bomber shot inside a crowd takes the crowd with it, its owner's units included. The known issue "a bomber hit on the tick it makes contact still explodes" ([known-issues.md](../../known-issues.md)) stops mattering and is closed.

**Aim for the middle, not the edge.**

- **Against units:** when a bomber's target is an enemy unit, it aims at a **blast point** instead of the unit: of the enemy units its side sees within `Bomber/AimTiles` (6) of the target, the position whose blast circle (radius 2) holds the most enemy worth (unit cost), less `Bomber/FriendlyWeight` (1.0) times the worth of its own side's and allies' units in it (the blast hurts friends). Ties go to the point nearest the bomber. The aim is re-chosen on the bomber's target-search slice, so it follows a moving army. A new per-unit array `BlastAim` (`SimData`, written by the combat stage's search and read by movement) steers the bomber there.
- **Detonates** when it is within `Bomber/AimReachTiles` (0.5) of the blast point, **or** when it touches any enemy and the worth in its blast where it stands is at least `Bomber/GoodEnough` (0.8) of the blast point's (so it doesn't push through a good group to reach a perfect one), **or** when it has made no progress towards the point for 1 s (blocked by bodies). It still dies by fire on the way, and the last-gasp rule then sets it off where it falls.
- **Against buildings:** blast damage to a building is **all or nothing** (`Detonate` gives the whole hit to every building with any part of its footprint in the radius), so where on its edge the bomber goes off doesn't change what the target takes. What it changes is what else is caught. A bomber whose target is a building runs to the **middle of a side**, never a corner: of the four points just outside the middle of each side, the one whose blast catches the most enemy worth (other buildings in the radius by their cost, plus units), ties to the side nearest the bomber. Walls are unchanged (contact with the tile).
- **The fog rule (leak):** a bomber's route reaches its owner's client, so the blast point is chosen only from enemy units and buildings its side **sees now**, the same as its target search, never from the server's full picture (`LeakTests.ABombersAimUsesOnlyWhatItsSideSees`).
- **Detonate (`B`)** and the AI's own detonation call (`BomberClump`, `BomberChecks`) are unchanged.

**Balance check:** bombers get stronger against massed armies and armies get more dangerous to defend with close-range units. Run `node tools/balance-sheet.js` and `tools/ai-bench.sh --brief` before landing; if the ladder or the bomber's value moves too far, the first knobs are `LastGaspHealth` and the bomber's cost, then `GoodEnough`. The AI's counter row for bombers (`<Counter seen="SuicideBomber">Tank:2 Sniper:1</Counter>`) may want more Snipers; that is part of the same check.

**Tests** (`SimBomberTests`): a bomber shot to 0 explodes where it fell and hurts both sides; a Digger's hit that leaves it at 2 health sets it off; decay sets it off; a chain of three bombers goes off in one tick and a fifth pass waits for the next tick; a bomber aimed at the edge of a 4-by-4 block of Tanks detonates in its middle and hits more of them than the nearest-edge rule did (a regression number checked in the test); it goes off early when its blast is already `GoodEnough`; it doesn't run through its own units' worth to reach a point; against an HQ it goes off at the middle of a side, never a corner; an enemy unit hidden in fog never moves its aim; the AI's detonation still works.

## 7. Rematch and remembered settings

### 7.1 Rematch

- **The host** (server owner) sees **Rematch** on the end screen, next to Main menu and Quit, once the replay is saved (or at once when recordings are off). Pressing it sends `Cmd_Rematch` (`CommandGate`; server owner, _GameOver_ only).
- **Everyone else** sees "Waiting for the host…" while the host decides, with Main menu and Quit as today; when the host presses Rematch their end screen says "Returning to the lobby" and the lobby opens.
- **On the server:** a reset like `GameCore.ResetToLobby`, but keeping players: the state goes back to _Lobby_; every connected human stays seated with their colour and team, ready cleared; **AI seats stay** with their callsign, difficulty, picked style (a Random style is drawn again at the next start) and team; the match settings stay except the seed, which is **rerolled** (the host can type the old one back); statistics, the timeline, teams, diplomacy and its cooldowns, pause, rejoin tickets, eliminated and away states, the squads kept by the server and every per-match server object are cleared (bans in `GameCore.Bans` are kept: a ban lasts while the server is open); the server changes scene back to `Main_Menu`, which disposes the match's ECS world and `SimData` as leaving does now. Humans who dropped during the match and didn't come back have no seat. The Steam lobby stays open with its name and visibility, and joining is allowed again (the in-match refusal no longer applies).
- **Clients:** every client-side match store (`ClientWorld`, `ClientUnitStore`, `ClientBuildings`, fog, decals, selection and squads, alerts) is cleared on the scene change, as leaving does; nothing from the first match may survive into the second. The rejoin store (`Selection.RememberForRejoin`) is dropped.
- **Tests:** PlayMode: host a match with an AI and a second client, end it, Rematch, start again, end again: the second match has fresh ids (`NetIdAllocator`), no units or buildings of the first, fresh fog and statistics, and its `LeakTests`-style checks pass; a client that pressed Main menu is gone from the lobby; Rematch is refused outside _GameOver_ and from a non-owner.

### 7.2 Remembered lobby settings

- When the host starts a match, the match settings (mode, diplomacy, map size, starting resources, options per player, HQ pick time, visibility, server name) and the AI seats (difficulty, style, team; not callsigns, drawn from the seed as now) are saved on the host's machine (`lobby.json` beside `settings.json`).
- **Hosting again** starts the lobby with them (each value checked against the config's lists, falling back to the default if it is no longer allowed); the seed is new. A **Reset** button among the host's settings restores the config defaults and removes the AIs. Joined players' machines save nothing.
- **Tests:** round-trip, a stale value falling back, Reset.
